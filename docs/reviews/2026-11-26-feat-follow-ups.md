One case would record the intended order.

## Strengths

- The audit path is transactional and race safe: `updateProfile` and `completeOnboarding` take `for update` on the business row, diff the locked before and after rows, and write the change and its audit rows in one transaction. A no op save writes nothing, and the integration suite proves it against a real database (21 cases, ran green here, including the repeat rejected with the profile and its single audit row intact).
- The shared `RedisRateLimitStore` handles a real outage with care: the Lua increment keeps the window on the first hit and repairs a lost TTL on 6.x and 7.x, the decrement never creates a key, the client is created lazily so `/health` probes open no socket, every command is bounded by a 500 ms timeout with offline queuing off, `/health` is skipped before the store is touched, and shutdown settles even when `quit()` rejects.
- Test breadth is honest rather than perfunctory: `rate-limit.redis.test.ts` drives two app instances, reads the hashed keys to prove the counts landed in Redis, and proves a successful login decrements it; `invite-rate-limit.redis.test.ts` proves the mutation limiter does not consume the invite quota; `business-audit.test.ts` covers the no op save, per field diffs, address add and edit, and logo set and clear; and the controller suite pins 401 for every unaudited write.
- The team mutation limiter sits after `requireBusinessRole(OWNER_ONLY)`, keys per owner rather than per IP, stays separate from the invite write quota, and the UI tells `RATE_LIMITED` from `LAST_OWNER` while keeping the confirm dialog open on failure.
- The rate limit flag is now read per request rather than frozen into a module boolean beside a `skipWhenDisabled` mirror, so the escape hatch has one expression and the lazily built limiters honour it after startup.
